0 · Why 40 × 30, not 36 × 30 supersedes v1

A 10-ft stick of EMT gives you bays in multiples of 10 ft. The old 36-ft number came from a tutorial's plot, not from a site constraint — and it forced 16 roof poles to be cut down to 9 ft for no structural reason. Going to 40 ft snaps the frame onto the material's natural grid: every roof pole is used full length, straight out of the bundle.

1 · Frame plan 5 × 4 node grid · 20 identical fittings

10' pole, 40' direction × 16 10' pole, 30' direction × 15 flat 5-way F5-1 × 20 (all identical) ▬ 2' offcut plug × 18
One fitting, twenty times. Corners, edges and interior nodes are all the same flat 5-way (F5-1). Nothing to sort, nothing to mix up in the dark, and a spare fits anywhere. The cost is that perimeter nodes have sockets pointing out into nothing — 18 of them (2 at each of the 4 corners, 1 at each of the 10 edge nodes). Those get capped with the 2-ft leg offcuts, which is why the offcuts ride to the playa instead of the recycler.
Legs: 20 poles cut to 8'-0", one under every node — including the 6 interior nodes. Interior legs are mandatory at this span; plan tents to shift ±1 ft to clear them (the ghost tents above are drawn around them). If your tallest tent exceeds 7'6", cut legs at 8'6" instead — same stick, smaller offcut, but then you only get 18" plugs.

Cut list — 54 sticks of 1" × 10' EMT

20 cuts total, all identical, all legs — set one stop block and run them production-style. Roughly 45 min for two people with tube cutters, ~15 min with a cordless angle grinder and a cut-off wheel. File or steel-wool every cut end. Scrap leaving the playa: 4 ft (the two spare plugs). Do not substitute 3/4" EMT — it's too whippy at a 10 ft span, and many cheap fitting kits are secretly sized for 3/4". Everything here is 1" (1.163" OD).

2 · Anchoring Decision open — pick before ordering

A 40×30 solid roof is a 1,200 sq ft sail. Anchoring is the difference between a structure and a kite, and the two candidate systems buy different things. This is not yet decided — both are costed in §5.

Unresolved · needs Bulat's call

Candy-cane rebar vs. lag bolts + climbing hangers

Option A — candy-cane rebar

14 anchors — one per perimeter node.

  • 3/8" × 2 ft J-hook stakes, fully buried, angled away from the structure, ~2 ft out.
  • Ratchet strap from the fitting body (never the eyebolts) down to the cane.
  • Cheapest, most familiar, no power tools.
  • Cost: trip hazard on all 14 — every one must be capped and lit.
  • Interior legs get a straight rebar pin lashed on, not an anchor.
Option B — lag bolts + climbing hangers

20 anchors — one per leg, interior legs included.

  • 3/8" × 14" lag bolts. NOT 1/2" × 16" — the climbing hanger hole is 10 mm and a 1/2" bolt physically will not pass through it.
  • Driver socket: 9/16", 6-point, deep impact. Bring a real impact wrench and spare batteries.
  • Sits flush — no trip hazard, clean removal, no MOOP.
  • Critical limit: lag bolts resist lateral sliding only. They give you almost no vertical hold-down — so each pole still needs its own vertical ratchet strap for uplift.

Open sub-question either way: diagonal ratchet straps across the roof bays for racking stiffness. Not yet specced, not yet costed.

Every stake capped and lit, every guy strap flagged. Rebar collisions are the #1 injury at the event — see Burning Man's rebar safety page and Securing Your Structure. Footplates spread load but do not hold the structure down — an unstaked frame is guaranteed to fly.

3 · Roof + east wing

Main roof — stock 30 × 40 tarp

The 40×30 frame lands on an off-the-shelf 30' × 40' tarp, which is the whole point of the dimension change. One honest caveat: poly tarps are sold at cut size and finish about 6" smaller per dimension, so a "30×40" arrives at roughly 29'6" × 39'6". That is ~3" shy on each edge of a true 40×30 frame — the tarp sits just inside the perimeter poles rather than wrapping over them. It bungees down fine and pulls taut; it just won't overhang. If you want a valance or any overhang at all, order the next size up and accept the extra flap.

East wing — 14 × 20 aluminet

A 14' × 20' double-layer aluminet panel angled off the roofline on the east side, for morning shade before the sun clears the roof. 280 sq ft. The 20-ft dimension is exactly 2 bays, so it hangs off two perimeter nodes with no improvising.

Two things to confirm on the wing. (1) Which axis faces east isn't specified anywhere in the brief — if the 40-ft side faces east the wing covers half of it; if the 30-ft side does, it covers 2 of 3 bays. (2) ShadeClothStore's site lists Aluminet Cool Shade in stock sizes and a custom program, but no catalogue "double-layer" product — that appears to be a custom-fabrication ask. Call it in rather than ordering blind: custom shade cloth, 866-727-6658.
Lead time is the real risk here. ShadeClothStore quotes 3–4 weeks for Aluminet custom orders. Gate opens Aug 30. Ordering today is already inside that window with no slack — this is the single most time-critical line on the sheet. If the quote comes back at 4 weeks, fall back to a stock-size Aluminet Cool Shade panel (ships next day) and adapt the mounting.

This week

3–4 week lead against an Aug 30 gate leaves no slack. Call ShadeClothStore (866-727-6658) before anything else on this list; if the lead time doesn't clear, switch to a stock-size panel that ships next day.

This week

Order fittings + soft goods to home

22 × F5-1 from Tarps.com (confirm stock by phone — the listing is ambiguous), tarp, bungees, cutter. Roughly 90 lb of boxes: pack into the vehicles, or ship to your Reno hotel — most hold packages for arriving guests. Anchoring hardware waits on the §2 decision, but don't let it wait long.

Aug 10–20

Dry-fit and label

With one fitting type there's nothing to sort — but still dry-fit one bay in a driveway to confirm the eyebolts seat on real EMT, coil straps, and bag bungees by panel. Ten minutes of prep saves an hour on playa.

Aug 20–26

Place the Reno pickup order

54 × EMT + tape + hammers, online for store pickup. Pickup orders are typically held about a week — confirm the window in your order email and call the Pro Desk so they stage 54 sticks.

Reno day (Aug 28–29)

Pick up steel + cutting party

Collect the conduit, then cut in the lot: 20 legs at 8'-0", nothing else. Deburr every end. Keep all 20 offcuts — 18 are your socket plugs. 10-ft sticks ride inside a 24-ft RV down the aisle, strapped.

On playa

Assemble

Lay the roof grid out flat and pin all 20 fittings → walk it up one side at a time onto legs (5–6 people at this size) → plug the 18 open sockets → anchor per §2 → east wing → cap and light every stake. Re-tighten every eyebolt, strap and stake daily. If a serious blow is forecast, dropping or rolling the roof tarp beats losing the frame.

Unresolved · needs Bulat's call

Does the tarp go on before or after the lift?

Tarp on while flat (v1 plan)

Bungee the tarp to the grid on the ground, then walk the whole assembly up.

  • No ladder work, no reaching over a 9-ft roof.
  • Much faster with a small crew.
  • Risk: the frame becomes a 1,200 sq ft sail during the lift, before a single anchor is in. One gust mid-lift and it's gone — with people under it.
Tarp last (reference guide)

Lift and fully anchor the bare frame, then put the roof on.

  • Bare frame has almost no wind area — the dangerous window disappears.
  • This is what the experienced-builder guide specifies.
  • Cost: ladders, more people, slower, awkward at 8-ft eave height.

The sequence above deliberately says "anchor → then wing" without resolving this. The safety argument favours tarp-last, and it's what the guide says, but it's Bulat's call and it changes the crew size and the day's schedule.

7 · Also flagged, not yet decided

Diagonal ratchet straps

Cross-bracing the roof bays against racking. Cheap insurance on a frame this wide, but not specced or costed. Needs a call on how many and where.

Sloped mesh side panels

Shade mesh on the SW (wind) faces, angled rather than vertical. Blocks low sun without turning the wall into a sail. Quantity depends on which sides the RVs already block.

Rain puddling

A flat single tarp ponds in a downpour, and a pond that size can pull the frame down. Mitigations: a slight built-in slope, a centre ridge stick, or just pushing water off with a broom. Unresolved.
